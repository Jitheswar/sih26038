1. Executive Summary

1.1 What we are building

A MATLAB-based retinal image analysis pipeline that screens fundus photographs for diabetic retinopathy at primary health centres in rural India, and that explains every decision it makes in terms a clinician can check in under thirty seconds.

The pipeline has five stages, matching the five requirements of SIH26038 one-to-one: an image quality gate that rejects ungradable captures and tells the operator how to retake them; a segmentation stage that finds the optic disc, fovea, vessels and the four lesion classes that define DR severity; a grading stage that outputs an International Clinical Diabetic Retinopathy (ICDR) severity level with a calibrated confidence; an explainability module that produces Grad-CAM attention maps and lesion-level clinical evidence and an annotated referral report; and a SimEvents discrete-event model of the district telemedicine programme that sizes grader and bandwidth capacity for a population of 100,000+ screened per year.

1.2 The distinctive claim

The problem statement says existing AI solutions "function as black boxes, lack clinical validation rigor, and fail with variable image quality from portable fundus cameras in field conditions." Those are three separate charges, and our design answers each with a concrete mechanism rather than a claim.

PS chargeOur mechanism
"function as black boxes" Two independent explanation channels, and a measured comparison between them. Grad-CAM (post-hoc, mandated by the PS) is benchmarked against inherently interpretable lesion-level evidence on the same images, scored quantitatively against IDRiD pixel-level lesion masks. Where the two disagree, the case is flagged as an unexplained prediction and routed to a human. Explanation stops being decoration and becomes a safety control.
"lack clinical validation rigor" Messidor-2 is sealed before development begins and opened exactly once, after the operating point has been frozen on a separate validation split. Results are reported at a pre-committed threshold with confidence intervals, per-class recall, and calibration error — never as bare accuracy. If external performance falls short of the PS targets, we report and analyse that rather than tuning until it looks good.
"fail with variable image quality" A quality gate runs before the classifier is permitted to predict, and can refuse. Every downstream metric is additionally reported stratified by quality tier, so the reader can see how performance degrades as capture conditions worsen instead of seeing a single number averaged over a clean subset.

1.4 What most competing teams will not build

Requirement 5 of the PS — the Simulink workflow simulation — is a fifth of the problem statement and is almost universally skipped or reduced to a static bar chart. It requires no GPU, no dataset and no machine learning. It is the single cheapest differentiator available, and it is the MathWorks-specific part of a MathWorks problem statement.

It is also genuinely interesting, because it closes a loop nothing else in the pipeline closes: the classifier's operating point is an input to the capacity model. Raising sensitivity produces more false positives, which consumes more grader hours, which lengthens turnaround. There is an optimum, and we can find it and plot it. See §9.

2.3 Reading R6.2 correctly

R6.2 is the requirement teams misread. "Outperforms any single-technique approach" is not rhetorical padding — it is an instruction to run an ablation study. You are being asked to build the components separately, measure them separately, then measure them combined, and show the combination wins.

This has a direct architectural consequence and it is why §11.6 exists. We must be able to evaluate, on the same held-out data:

  1. Classifier alone, no quality gate, no lesion evidence.
  2. Classifier with quality gate.
  3. Lesion-rule grading alone (ICDR criteria applied to detected lesion counts, no CNN).
  4. Full integrated pipeline: quality gate + classifier + lesion evidence + deferral.

Configuration 3 will almost certainly be the weakest on raw accuracy, and that is fine — its purpose is to be the interpretable reference, and its disagreements with configuration 1 are what drive the unexplained-prediction flag. The pipeline must therefore be built so that any stage can be switched off from a config file rather than by editing code. See §13.3.

3. Clinical Foundations

You cannot defend a grading model without knowing the grading rules it is imitating. This section is the minimum clinical literacy every team member needs, and it is where most of the judge questions will come from.

3.1 What diabetic retinopathy is

Chronic hyperglycaemia damages the small blood vessels of the retina. The vessel walls weaken and leak, then occlude, starving areas of retina of oxygen. In response the retina releases growth factors that drive the formation of new, fragile vessels. Those vessels bleed into the vitreous and pull on the retina as they contract, which is the mechanism of severe vision loss.

The clinically important consequence for us: DR is graded by counting and locating lesions, not by looking at the whole image holistically. That is why a lesion-grounded explanation is not merely a nice presentation choice — it mirrors the actual diagnostic procedure a human grader performs. Established

The microaneurysm resolution problem — read this before choosing any input size

A microaneurysm can be as small as ~10 µm. In a fundus photograph, that is on the order of a handful of pixels at native camera resolution, and it becomes sub-pixel at the input sizes CNNs conventionally use.

Consider what actually happens with a standard pipeline. An IDRiD image is 4288×2848. Resize it to the ResNet-50 default of 224×224 and you have shrunk it by a factor of roughly 19 in each dimension. A 20-pixel microaneurysm becomes approximately one pixel. It is not that the model finds this hard — the evidence has been destroyed before the model ever sees it.

This single fact drives three decisions later in this document: the classifier operates at high input resolution (§7.2), lesion segmentation is patch-based at native resolution rather than on downsampled whole images (§6.4), and Grad-CAM's coarse output is explicitly acknowledged as insufficient for MA localisation rather than being oversold (§8.3).

Notice what the rule is made of: counts of specific lesions within specific spatial regions. That is precisely what a lesion segmentation model produces. This means our lesion-evidence channel (§8.4) is not an approximation of clinical reasoning invented for a hackathon — it is a direct computational restatement of the published grading criterion. When a judge asks "how do you know your explanation is clinically meaningful?", the answer is: because it is expressed in the same terms as the diagnostic standard, and here is the rule it evaluates.

3.4 Referable DR: the endpoint that actually matters

The PS sets its performance targets on referable DR, defined as Level 2 or above. This matches international screening practice and is the endpoint used by regulator-cleared autonomous systems. The screening question is not "what grade is this?" but "does this person need to see an ophthalmologist?"

This asymmetry must shape every evaluation decision we make:

4.2 The three-way decision, and why deferral is a first-class output

Most DR classifiers emit two outcomes: refer or do not refer. Ours emits three.

Escalate
(human grading required)		Model uncertainty above threshold, or Grad-CAM and lesion evidence disagree, or image quality borderlineImage queued for remote human grader. This is the human-in-the-loop path the PS requires under R4.5.

Design rationale: the "unexplained prediction" flag

This is the mechanism we consider the most defensible original contribution in the design, and it is worth being able to explain fluently.

If the CNN predicts referable DR but the lesion detector finds no microaneurysms, no haemorrhages and no exudates, then one of two things is true: either the lesion detector missed something, or the classifier is keying on something that is not disease. The second case is a well-documented failure mode in retinal imaging — models learn camera artefacts, image borders, and laterality rather than pathology.

Either way, the case is one a human should see. Standard post-hoc XAI would generate a heatmap for this prediction and present it as a justification, which is actively misleading. Our design detects the condition and refuses to auto-decide. This converts explainability from a presentation layer into a runtime safety control, and it is measurable: §11.7 defines the disagreement-rate metric.

5. Component 1 — Image Quality Assessment and Enhancement

Discharges R1.1, R1.2, R1.3. This is the component to build first: it requires no training, no GPU and no labelled data, and it is the PS's own stated differentiator against existing solutions.

5.1 Why this comes first

A deployed Google DR screening system in Thai clinics rejected 393 of 1,838 images (21%) for quality over the first six months of use, a finding reported in a human-centred evaluation study at CHI 2020. Verified The causes were mundane and physical: fundus photos taken in non-darkened rooms, cameras needing repair, and clinics not using dilation drops. Context, so this is not overstated: the fieldwork covered 11 clinics across Pathum Thani and Chiang Mai, but the system was deployed in three Pathum Thani clinics, and the 21% is a log-derived rate over those deployed sites. The general lesson is robust and is exactly what the PS asserts: models that perform well on curated datasets degrade in the field primarily because of image quality, not because of the model.

Building the gate first also has a practical benefit for a four-day demo: it is entirely classical computer vision, so it produces a working, demonstrable stage on day one while the classifier is still training.

5.2 Quality metrics (R1.1)

All metrics are computed inside the field-of-view mask only. This matters: the black surround of a fundus image would otherwise dominate every intensity statistic and make the measures meaningless.

Step 1 — Field-of-view mask

Threshold the image to separate the illuminated circular retina from the black border, take the largest connected component, fill holes, and fit a circle. The mask is reused by every downstream stage, so compute it once and cache it.

% Approximate structure; tune thresholds per camera
gray = im2gray(I);
bw   = imbinarize(gray, 0.05);        % retina vs black surround
bw   = imfill(bw, 'holes');
bw   = bwareafilt(bw, 1);             % largest component only
stats = regionprops(bw, 'Centroid', 'EquivDiameter', 'BoundingBox');
fovMask   = bw;
fovRadius = stats.EquivDiameter / 2;

5.3 Quality classifier

The features above feed a small classifier producing three classes: gradable, borderline (enhance and retry), ungradable (reject).

Use fitcecoc or a decision tree from Statistics and Machine Learning Toolbox rather than a neural network. Three reasons, and they are all worth being able to say out loud:

  1. The feature set is small and hand-designed, so a deep model has nothing to add.
  2. A decision tree is itself interpretable, which is consistent with the project's thesis. The quality gate can state which feature caused a rejection, and that is precisely what generates the recapture message in §5.5.
  3. It trains on a few hundred hand-labelled examples in seconds on CPU.

Labels. Hand-label roughly 300–400 APTOS images into the three tiers. This is a two-hour task for two people and needs no clinical training — "can you see the retina clearly enough to look for small red dots?" is the operative question. Record the labelling protocol in the repository so it is reproducible.

5.4 Enhancement (R1.2)

Applied only to borderline images. Order matters.

#OperationDetail
1Illumination normalisationEstimate the slowly varying background with a large-kernel filter and subtract it, then re-centre. Removes vignetting and uneven flash without touching lesion-scale detail.
2CLAHEadapthisteq applied to the green channel, or to the L channel in Lab space to preserve colour relationships. Contrast-limited so it does not amplify noise into false lesions.
3Denoisingimnlmfilt (non-local means). Applied conservatively — over-denoising erases microaneurysms, which are exactly the small dark dots a denoiser wants to remove.

Self-inflicted domain shift — the most likely silent bug in this component

If enhancement is applied at inference but not during training, you have created a distribution mismatch with your own hands, and the model will underperform for a reason no metric will name. Rule: the enhancement decision is part of the pipeline, and the pipeline is identical at training and inference time. Both must run the same gate and apply the same enhancement to the same category of image. Encode this by having exactly one preprocessing function, called from both paths. Never two.

6. Component 2 — Retinal Structure Segmentation

Discharges R2.1–R2.6. This is the component that makes lesion-level explanation possible, and it is also where the honest limits of the available data must be stated.

6.1 Two-track strategy

Every structure below is addressed on two tracks, and this is a deliberate risk-management decision rather than indecision:

TrackNaturePurpose
Track A — ClassicalMorphology, filtering, geometric priors. No training.Works on day one. Provides a functioning demo before any model has converged, and remains the fallback if a segmentation model fails to train usefully on small data.
Track B — LearnedPatch-based semantic segmentation networks trained on IDRiD and DRIVE.Higher ceiling. The version presented at the grand finale.

Track A is not a placeholder to be embarrassed about. Classical morphological microaneurysm detection is a genuine, long-established line of work in retinal image analysis, and having a non-learned baseline to compare against is itself part of the ablation R6.2 demands.

6.2 Optic disc and fovea localisation (R2.1)

Track A. The optic disc is the brightest large structure in a normal fundus image and is the point of vessel convergence. Locate candidate bright regions of approximately disc size within the FOV, then disambiguate using vessel density, since vessels radiate from the disc.

The fovea is then found geometrically. It sits approximately 2.5 disc diameters temporal to the disc centre, roughly on the horizontal axis through the disc. Established Refine by searching that neighbourhood for the darkest avascular region.

Track B. IDRiD provides optic disc masks and fovea centre coordinates, so a small U-Net for the disc and a coordinate-regression head for the fovea are both directly trainable.

Why we need these at all. Three downstream uses, and a judge may well ask: field-definition checking in the quality gate (§5.2); quadrant definition for the 4-2-1 rule, which is defined relative to the disc-fovea axis (§8.5); and exudate disambiguation, since the optic disc is bright and yellow and is the classic false positive for hard exudate detection.

6.3 Vessel segmentation (R2.2)

Dataset: DRIVE, 40 images with expert vessel annotations, split 20 train / 20 test. Verified — this is the dataset's own defined split, not merely a convention. One provenance point to state explicitly: the official challenge site withholds test-set annotations and expects online submission, whereas the commonly circulated DRIVE archive ships first- and second-observer manual segmentations for the test images. If we score the test set locally, say which annotations we used.

Approach: patch-based U-Net on the green channel. Forty images is very few for whole-image training, but extracting overlapping patches yields tens of thousands of training samples, which is ample. This is the standard approach for DRIVE and is why a 40-image dataset remains usable.

Metrics: sensitivity, specificity and AUC against the expert masks, which are the conventional DRIVE benchmark measures and let us compare to published results as R6.1 requires.

Downstream uses: venous beading assessment for the 4-2-1 rule; vessel masking to suppress false lesion detections, since dark vessel segments are readily mistaken for haemorrhages; and neovascularisation features (§6.6).

6.4 Microaneurysm detection (R2.3) — the hard one

Set expectations honestly here

The PS asks for "sub-pixel microaneurysm detection." Take that phrase seriously rather than treating it as flourish: it is telling you that MAs are at or below the effective resolution of a naively downsampled pipeline. IDRiD's segmentation subset provides 81 images with MA masks, split 54 train (Set-A) / 27 test (Set-B). Verified This is a very small training set for a very small object. Plan for this to be the component that most nearly fails, and build Track A so that it cannot fail completely.

Track A — morphological candidate detection

The classical pipeline, which needs no training data at all:

  1. Take the inverted green channel, where MAs appear as small bright peaks.
  2. Apply morphological top-hat with linear structuring elements at multiple orientations. Elongated structures (vessels) survive at least one orientation and are suppressed; small round structures (MAs) do not, and are retained. This is the key trick and it exploits the shape difference directly.
  3. Threshold to obtain candidates.
  4. Extract per-candidate features: area, eccentricity, peak intensity, local contrast, distance to nearest vessel.
  5. Classify candidates as MA or not with a small model from Statistics and Machine Learning Toolbox.

Track B — patch-based segmentation at native resolution

Capture scale is a transfer variable, and it is not small

A lesion network learns objects at the pixel scale of the frames it trained on. IDRiD is one camera at one protocol: measured over ten Set-A frames the illuminated field is 3279 pixels across and varies by three pixels over the whole sample Verified. APTOS is not one camera. Measured over twelve frames its field ranges from 1055 to 2555 pixels Verified, so the same physical lesion covers between a third and four fifths as many pixels there as the network was trained to expect.

Feeding APTOS to an IDRiD-trained lesion network unchanged therefore asks it to find objects at a scale it never saw, and nothing in a per-frame output would report that it had happened. segment.segmentLesions resamples every frame so its field diameter matches reference_fov_diameter before tiling, and maps the probability maps back to the caller's pixel grid afterwards; the factor applied is recorded in the result metadata. For an IDRiD frame the factor is 1.00 and the step is a no-op, so the Set-B benchmark number below is unaffected by it.

State the residual limitation rather than hiding behind the fix: resampling presents lesions at the learned scale, it does not create detail a 1050×1050 capture never recorded. A microaneurysm a few pixels across at IDRiD resolution is not present in that frame at any scale factor. This bounds what the microaneurysm head can achieve on the smaller APTOS captures, and it is a further reason — beyond §6.4's — to keep the classical Track A detector in the pipeline.

6.6 Neovascularisation (R2.6) — declared data gap

State this plainly rather than pretending

None of the four datasets named in the PS provides pixel-level neovascularisation annotations. IDRiD supplies image-level grades that include proliferative DR, and it supplies pixel masks for MA, HE, EX and SE — but not for NV. We therefore cannot train a supervised NV segmenter on the named data, and we will not claim to have done so.

What we do instead, and say we do:

  1. Image-level PDR detection via the classifier. Level 4 is one of the five ICDR classes the grading model outputs, so proliferative disease is detected at image level even without lesion-level NV masks.
  2. Vessel-morphology proxy features. From the vessel segmentation, compute abnormal branching density, tortuosity and calibre irregularity, particularly within one disc diameter of the optic disc where disc neovascularisation characteristically occurs. Report these as supporting indicators, explicitly not as a validated NV detector.
  3. Route to a human. Any predicted Level 4 is escalated for human grading regardless of confidence. Proliferative DR is sight-threatening and comparatively rare, so the cost of always escalating it is small and the cost of an autonomous miss is severe.

This is the correct engineering answer and the correct presentation answer. A judge who knows these datasets knows there are no NV masks in them. A team that claims a trained NV segmenter is either mistaken or has quietly used data they have not declared. A team that names the gap, explains the mitigation, and escalates the risky class is demonstrating exactly the "clinical validation rigor" the PS says the field lacks.

7.6 Probability calibration (R4.3)

The PS asks for "calibrated confidence scores." Raw softmax output is not calibrated — modern neural networks are systematically overconfident. Presenting a softmax value as a confidence is one of the most common unforced errors in student ML projects, and it is easy to get right.

Method

  1. Hold out a calibration split, distinct from both the training set and the test set.
  2. Fit a single temperature parameter T that minimises negative log-likelihood on that split, dividing the logits by T before the softmax.
  3. Report Expected Calibration Error before and after, and include a reliability diagram in the results.

Temperature scaling is a one-parameter fit, takes minutes, and produces a defensible answer to "what does 0.87 mean?" — namely that among cases where the system reports 0.87, close to 87% genuinely are referable. That sentence, backed by a reliability diagram, is a strong judging moment.

8.3 The Grad-CAM resolution limitation — know this number

Grad-CAM computes its map at the spatial resolution of the chosen convolutional layer, then upsamples. For ResNet-50, the final convolutional block has a stride of 32 relative to the input. At 448×448 input, the Grad-CAM map is 14×14. Each cell of that map therefore covers roughly a 32×32-pixel region of the resized input.

A microaneurysm at that scale occupies a fraction of one cell. Grad-CAM at the final layer physically cannot localise a microaneurysm. This is not a tuning problem; it is arithmetic.

What we do about it, and what we say about it:

  1. Compute Grad-CAM at multiple depths. Earlier layers give finer maps (lower stride) but weaker class specificity. Present the trade-off explicitly rather than silently picking one. Selecting the layer is a gradCAM argument, so this is inexpensive.
  2. Do not oversell it. Grad-CAM answers "which region drove this decision," and for large lesions such as exudates and haemorrhages it does that well. Report it as regional attention, not lesion localisation.
  3. Supply channel 2 for the fine detail. This is precisely why the PS asks for lesion-level evidence in the same breath.
  4. Measure the gap rather than asserting it (§11.7).

Being able to state this limitation crisply is worth more in front of a MathWorks judge than any accuracy figure in the deck. It demonstrates that you understand the tool rather than having invoked it.

  • Count detections per lesion class per quadrant.
  • Record the distance of each hard exudate from the fovea, since exudates near the macula carry different clinical significance.
  • Render as coloured overlays with a legend and a counts table.

8.6 The agreement check and the unexplained-prediction flag

With three channels we can cross-examine the system's own output. Four states:

StateConditionAction
ConcordantCNN grade and rule-path grade agree; Grad-CAM peaks coincide with detected lesionsProceed with the automated decision. Report high explanation confidence.
Under-detectedCNN says referable; lesion detector finds little or nothingEscalate. Either the detector missed findings or the classifier is keying on something that is not disease.

Spatially inconsistentGrades agree, but Grad-CAM attention falls outside all detected lesionsFlag in the report as reduced explanation confidence. Consider escalation.

The under-detected state is the important one. Retinal classifiers are known to be able to exploit non-pathological image properties, and a model that has learned a camera artefact will still produce a confident prediction and a plausible-looking heatmap. Standard post-hoc XAI presents that heatmap as a justification, which is worse than offering no explanation at all. Our pipeline detects the condition and declines to decide.

This is an upgrade, not a patch. An adjudicated three-specialist consensus is a stronger reference standard than the single-rating grades in our training data. Second, whichever grade set we use must be named in the submission, because external results are not comparable across grade sets — that is the entire point of the Krause et al. paper we would be citing.

Messidor-2 is distributed under a licence agreement rather than by direct download, and IDRiD is hosted on IEEE DataPort. Both can take days to clear. Request access on day one. A sealed external test set you cannot obtain until December is not a validation strategy. If Messidor-2 access does not arrive in time, the fallback is a held-out, patient-disjoint IDRiD split as the external set, clearly labelled as a weaker substitute because it shares an Indian origin with the training data.

10.4 The sealed test set — the core rigour mechanism

Protocol

  1. On download, Messidor-2 is placed in an archive that is encrypted or otherwise access-controlled, and one named team member holds the key. It is not extracted into the working tree.
  2. All development — architecture selection, hyperparameter tuning, threshold selection, calibration — happens using train / validation / calibration splits only.
  3. When the team judges the system complete, the operating point is frozen and recorded in this document with a date, along with the validation-set metrics that justified it.
  4. Only then is the sealed set opened, and the frozen pipeline run over it exactly once.
  5. Whatever comes out is reported. If external performance falls below the PS targets, that result is reported and analysed, not tuned away.
  6. If any change is made to the pipeline after opening the seal, the resulting number ceases to be an external validation result and must be reported as such.

Two reasons this is worth the discipline. The scientific one: any threshold chosen with knowledge of the test set makes the reported figure an optimistic estimate of deployed performance, and screening decisions affect eyesight. The competitive one: the PS explicitly criticises existing solutions for lacking validation rigour. A team that can describe this protocol, and show the frozen operating point with a date preceding the test run, has answered the problem statement's central complaint with a procedure rather than a claim.

We predict, before running the experiment, that performance on Messidor-2 will be lower than on the internal APTOS test split. Recording the prediction here, in advance, is part of the protocol.

11.7 Explanation quality metrics — the differentiator

Almost every team will show heatmaps. Very few will measure them. IDRiD's pixel-level lesion masks make quantitative evaluation possible, and this is the main reason IDRiD is in our pipeline at all.

MetricDefinition and purpose
Lesion hit rate (pointing game)Take the top-k% most salient pixels of the explanation map. What fraction falls inside a ground-truth lesion mask? Computed identically for Grad-CAM, occlusion sensitivity, LIME and the lesion-evidence channel. This is the head-to-head comparison the PS's R6.2 asks for, made numeric.
Deletion / insertion AUCProgressively remove the pixels the explanation says matter most and track the predicted probability. A faithful explanation causes a fast drop (deletion) and a fast rise (insertion). Measures faithfulness to the model, which is distinct from agreement with clinical ground truth — report both, because an explanation can be faithful to a model that is wrong.
Sanity checks (parameter randomisation)Progressively randomise the trained model's weights from the top layer down and recompute the explanation. If the map barely changes, the method is not explaining this model — it is largely an edge detector. Verified In Adebayo et al., Guided BackProp and Guided GradCAM fail (invariant to higher-layer parameters), while plain Gradients and Grad-CAM pass. Grad-CAM — the method the PS mandates and we ship — is on the passing side, and we should say so.

The more interesting case is Gradient×input and Integrated Gradients: they change quantitatively under randomisation (signed rank correlation falls to zero) while remaining visually convincing. That is exactly our failure mode — a heatmap that looks right and means nothing — and it is the reason we measure rather than eyeball. Citation care: the NeurIPS 2018 camera-ready says Guided BackProp is entirely invariant; arXiv v3 corrects this to invariant to higher-layer parameters after a bug fix. Cite the venue, state the corrected wording.

Running this on your own maps is a strong rigour signal and costs one afternoon.
Channel disagreement rateFraction of cases in each of the four states of §8.6. Quantifies how often the unexplained-prediction flag fires, which is both a safety statistic and a direct input to §9.5 E3.

12. Clinical Reader Study Protocol

Discharges R4.5 and R6.3, and supplies the grader service-time distribution to §9.4. This is the highest-value-per-hour activity in the project and it requires one email.

12.1 Why it is worth the effort

R6.3 requires Grad-CAM outputs "rated as clinically useful," and R4.5 requires "ophthalmologist validation in under 30 seconds." Neither can be discharged by an automated metric. Both require a clinician looking at your outputs and a stopwatch.

The PLOS Digital Health study cited in §1.3 is essentially this experiment at scale, with six ophthalmologists. A twenty-case pilot with one ophthalmologist is entirely achievable by two students in an afternoon, and it converts two requirements from assertions into measurements.

12.2 Recruitment

You need one ophthalmologist for approximately two hours. A postgraduate resident in ophthalmology at any government medical college is an appropriate and reachable participant. Approach the department by email, state that this is a student project for a national hackathon, that no patient data is involved (all images are from public research datasets), and that you need two hours. Offer acknowledgement in the submission.

12.3 Design

Cases20–30, stratified across ICDR levels, and deliberately including cases where the channels of §8.6 disagree
Reference standardDataset ground-truth grades
Arms(1) image only; (2) image + AI grade; (3) image + AI grade + full explanation
OrderingCounterbalanced across cases so that arm order cannot explain any effect; the same case is not seen twice in the same session
Primary measuresTime to decision (target: under 30 s, per R4.5); agreement with reference standard
Secondary measuresExplanation usefulness on a Likert scale; free-text comment on each; explicit flag for any explanation judged misleading

Record the misleading cases especially carefully

An explanation rated misleading is a more valuable finding than one rated useful, and it is exactly the failure mode the unexplained-prediction flag (§8.6) exists to catch. If the clinician flags a case as misleading, check whether your agreement check had already flagged it. If it had, that is a validated safety mechanism and it belongs in the presentation. If it had not, that is a real finding about your system's limits, and reporting it is what rigour looks like.

12.4 Honest reporting

With a single reader and twenty cases this is a pilot, not a clinical validation study. Report it as a pilot: state n, state that it is one reader, give confidence intervals, and do not generalise. A correctly-labelled pilot is credible and impressive for a student team. A twenty-case study described as "clinical validation" invites a judge to dismantle it.

12.5 Fallback if no clinician is available

If nobody responds before the finale, do not fabricate ratings. Instead: present the protocol above as a designed, ready-to-run study; report the automated proxies from §11.7 in its place; and state plainly that clinical rating is pending. A well-specified unexecuted protocol is a legitimate deliverable. Invented clinician ratings are misconduct, and they are also easy to catch, because a judge will ask which hospital and which clinician.

17. Known Limitations and Honest Disclosures

This section exists because the problem statement's central complaint is that existing solutions lack validation rigour. A limitations section that names real constraints is a direct answer to that complaint. Present it; do not bury it.

17.1 Neovascularisation

No pixel-level NV annotations exist in any of the four named datasets. We detect proliferative DR at image level through the classifier and use vessel-morphology proxy features as supporting indicators only. Every predicted Level 4 is escalated to a human regardless of confidence. We do not claim a validated NV detector. (§6.6)

17.2 Single-field imaging

Our pipeline grades single fundus photographs. The historical clinical reference standard for DR grading is seven-field stereoscopic photography. Single-field screening is standard practice and is what portable cameras produce, but it sees less retina, and peripheral pathology can be missed. This limits the ceiling on achievable sensitivity independently of model quality. Established

17.3 Label noise

APTOS labels are described by the organisers as a single clinician's rating per image ("A clinician has rated each image..."), with no adjudication process documented either way, and Kaggle explicitly warns that "you will encounter noise in both the images and labels." Verified Stated carefully on purpose: the organisers never say adjudication did not happen, so we should not claim it did not. Inter-grader agreement on DR severity is imperfect even among specialists, so a portion of apparent model error is label disagreement rather than model failure. This particularly affects the Level 1 versus Level 2 boundary — which is unfortunately our referral threshold. Report 5-class confusion matrices so adjacent-grade confusions are visible rather than hidden inside an aggregate. Established

17.4 Not a medical device

This is a screening aid and a research prototype. It has not been through any regulatory process, has no CDSCO clearance, and must not be used for clinical decision-making. Every generated report carries this statement in its footer (§8.7). Say this in the presentation too — it signals that you understand the domain rather than diminishing the work.

17.5 Diabetic macular oedema not addressed

The PS scopes the task to the ICDR severity scale and does not require DME detection. Clinically, DME is a distinct and important cause of vision loss and is part of a complete referral decision. Our hard-exudate detection with fovea-distance measurement (§8.4) provides a partial proxy, but this is not a DME classifier. Named here so the scope boundary is explicit.

17.6 Reader study scale

Planned as a single-reader, ~20-case pilot. Reported as a pilot with confidence intervals, never as clinical validation. (§12.4)

17.7 Dataset representativeness

APTOS and IDRiD are Indian, which is favourable for our deployment target, but neither was collected under the field conditions the PS describes — handheld cameras, uncontrolled lighting, untrained operators. Our quality gate is designed for those conditions but is validated on data that largely does not contain them. Closing this gap requires field data we do not have.

17.8 Simulation is not deployment

The SimEvents model uses parameters partly measured from our own pipeline (rejection rate, inference latency, grader time) and partly assumed (arrival patterns, bandwidth, connectivity). It is a capacity-planning tool whose conclusions are conditional on those assumptions. State the assumptions alongside every result it produces.
